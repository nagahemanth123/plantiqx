## 2. The gap PlantiqX can own

| Axis | Where competitors sit (observed) | Where PlantiqX sits |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | One module each. PdM players do machines, VMS players do the visitor, freight players do freight, vision players do cameras. | Five modules on one login and one data layer |
| Customer size | PdM and vision leaders sell to steel, cement and fertiliser majors (JSW, Tata Steel, IFFCO). Freight platforms sell to large shippers. | Mid-size plants, one site to many |
| Hardware | Own sensors for one job (vibration, cameras) | Custom IoT, embedded panels and gateways retrofitted to old machines |

**The line to own:** *the gate, the machines, the cameras, the meters and the freight desk of a mid-size plant on one login, retrofitted onto the machines it already has, with a team that builds the sensor or panel when nothing off the shelf fits.*

Why it is credible: the same Infinite Uptime / MIT SMR India study finds 62% of plants keep machine and process data scattered across systems (section 3). That is the one-platform argument, in a competitor's own research.

Closest threats (inferred): **Faclon Labs** (energy + machines + utilities in one, mid-market tone) and **Altizon** (platform story). **Elmeasure** is more likely a partner than a rival, since its meters are already on panels PlantiqX would read over Modbus.

What PlantiqX still has to prove (hypotheses until it has numbers): uptime gained, rupees saved on energy and freight, gate time saved, from the two live VEMS clients and the furnace project.
